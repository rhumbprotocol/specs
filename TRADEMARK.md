## 1. Summary

This policy defines how the Rhumb marks may be used, under what conditions, and what mark-use obligations attach to claims of Rhumb-compliance. It applies to every party that references the Rhumb marks in a product, project, service, domain, social handle, marketing material, technical documentation, academic publication, or any other public communication.

The Rhumb Workflow Protocol™ (RWP™) is open-source software distributed under Apache License 2.0. The license grants broad rights over the protocol's source code, schemas, templates, grammar, and reference implementations. The license does **not** grant rights over the Rhumb marks. Trademark rights are governed exclusively by this policy. Apache 2.0 §6 makes this separation explicit; this document fills the space that the license intentionally leaves empty.

### 4.3 What the rights provide

For both common-law marks (§4.2) and the pending-application period of the filed marks (§4.1, until registration grants), the rights are:

- a documented first-use-in-commerce date that anchors priority of use in any future registration proceeding,
- regional rights in the United States in commerce zones where the marks are used,
- standing to bring state-court actions for passing-off or unfair competition,
- authority to issue cease-and-desist correspondence against parties using a confusingly similar mark.

Common-law rights do **not** provide federal-court jurisdiction, statutory damages, presumption of validity, or international protection. The §4.1 pending applications, if granted, will provide nationwide constructive-use priority dating to the filing date and the additional remedies that attach to registered marks.

The trigger gates in §7 are the conditions under which the gap between common-law and federal rights becomes worth closing. For the marks listed in §4.1, the pending-application filings already address that gap (subject to examination and registration); the §7 framework remains active for the marks listed in §4.2.

## 6. Mark-Use Policy — When You May Use the Marks

The following rules describe what uses of the Rhumb marks are permitted, what uses are conditional, and what uses are forbidden.

### 6.1 Reference use (permitted freely)

Reference use is permitted without prior permission. Reference use means using the Rhumb marks in their plain, factual sense — to refer to the protocol itself — in:

- technical documentation, books, blog posts, conference talks, podcasts, academic papers, comparison articles, tutorials,
- internal corporate documentation discussing the protocol,
- factual descriptive labels on tools, libraries, or services that interoperate with RWP (for example, "this library reads Rhumb Workflow Protocol™ artifacts"),
- the NOTICE file required by Apache License 2.0 §4(d).

Reference use should display ™ on the first prominent occurrence of each mark in any given document or surface, and should use the marks as adjectives rather than nouns where natural ("a Rhumb Workflow Protocol™ artifact," not "a Rhumb").

### 6.2 Compliance claim (conditional)

The phrase "Rhumb-compliant," and equivalent phrases such as "RWP-compliant," "compliant with the Rhumb Workflow Protocol," or "passes the Rhumb conformance suite," may be used **only** by implementations whose artifacts pass the official conformance check.

The official conformance check is `rhumb-validate --all` exiting with status code `0`. This is the operational definition of compliance. No other test, internal audit, self-assessment, or third-party review qualifies an implementation to use a compliance claim.

The `rhumb-validate` tool is open source and ships as part of the RWP project. Any party may run it against any artifact set at any time. Compliance claims are therefore independently verifiable — a publisher of a compliance claim implicitly invites audit by anyone.

A compliance claim made by an implementation that does not pass `rhumb-validate --all` is a misrepresentation. YAKKL®, Inc. reserves all remedies available to it under trademark law, false-advertising law, and unfair-competition law.

### 6.3 Attribution claim (conditional)

Attribution phrases such as "implements the Rhumb Workflow Protocol™," "supports RWP™," or "built on the Rhumb Workflow Protocol™" are conditional on the same gate as §6.2. An implementation that does not pass `rhumb-validate --all` may not attribute itself to the protocol in marketing or product copy. It may, of course, describe its work factually (for example, "this project began as an exploration of the Rhumb Workflow Protocol™ but does not currently pass conformance"); the line being drawn here is between factual description and origin/endorsement claims.

### 6.4 Product-naming use (requires written agreement)

The Rhumb marks may **not** be used as the primary identifier in the name of any product, project, organization, service, repository, package, container image, domain, social handle, or commercial offering, without a prior written agreement with YAKKL®, Inc.

Examples of uses that require written agreement:

- naming a product `RhumbX`, `Rhumb Studio`, `Rhumb Cloud`, or `RhumbHub`,
- registering a domain such as `rhumbprotocol.io`, `rwp-cloud.com`, or `rhumb.dev` for a commercial service,
- creating a GitHub organization or NPM/crates scope using the marks (for example, `@rhumb/...` outside the official scope),
- launching a SaaS service whose name contains the marks (for example, `rhumb-as-a-service.com`).

The bar for written agreement is "non-confusing use that does not suggest official endorsement, sponsorship, or origin from YAKKL®, Inc., and that does not undermine the conformance gate in §6.2." The agreement is not the same thing as a commercial license; non-commercial uses, academic projects, and non-profit deployments are equally subject to the agreement requirement, and equally welcome to enquire. YAKKL®, Inc. aims to grant written agreements liberally for community projects, conferences, books, and non-confusing commercial uses, and to refuse only those uses that create confusion or suggest unauthorized affiliation.

This sub-section is the strictest part of the policy and is intentionally so. Product-naming use is the surface on which trademark law most directly operates, and the surface where uncontrolled use causes the most damage to the protocol's coherence and to its long-term viability.

### 6.5 Forks and derivatives

Apache License 2.0 permits forking and modification of the source materials. This policy permits forks to:

- exist, be distributed, and be used,
- describe themselves factually (for example, "a fork of the Rhumb Workflow Protocol™ source materials"),
- carry NOTICE files reproducing required attributions.

This policy does **not** permit forks to:

- describe themselves as "Rhumb," "RWP," "Rhumb Workflow Protocol™," or any visually or phonetically similar name as their primary identifier,
- represent themselves as "Rhumb-compliant" unless they pass `rhumb-validate --all` per §6.2,
- imply endorsement, sponsorship, or maintenance by YAKKL®, Inc.

A fork that materially changes the protocol's artifact shapes, schemas, grammar, or templates must use a distinct name. The mark-use boundary here is not punitive — it is the mechanism that protects users of "Rhumb" software from receiving software that does not behave the way the Rhumb marks promise it will.

## 7. ® Trigger Gates (KD-03b)

> **Scope note (added 2026-05-02 in document version 1.1.0)**: The triggers below were drafted to govern the ™-to-® transition for all five marks listed in §2. As of 2026-04-30, two of those marks ("Rhumb Workflow Protocol", "RWP") are subject to pending USPTO Section 1(b) intent-to-use applications (see §4.1) and the trigger framework no longer governs their registration timeline — examination and prosecution under existing serial numbers does. The triggers remain in force for the three marks listed in §4.2 (the bare "Rhumb" mark, the rhumbprotocol.com brand mark, and the Rhumb logo / wordmark / visual identity), which remain on common-law ™ posture. KD-03b is preserved verbatim below for evidentiary continuity.

The following triggers, from YAKKL's internal architecture record AVD-0004 §KD-03b (decision dated 2026-04-18), are the conditions under which YAKKL®, Inc. will file USPTO federal registration for the Rhumb marks. **Any one** trigger firing is sufficient — the triggers do not need to combine. The first trigger that fires will spawn a new internal MP for USPTO filing plus attorney engagement.

The triggers are reproduced below verbatim from KD-03b.

**Trigger 1**. A third party publicly announces a competing "Rhumb" mark in software, protocol, or AI class (1, 9, 42).

> *Rationale*. A competing public announcement creates an actual collision. Common-law rights in the United States are regional; a federal registration converts the geographic scope to nationwide and supplies a constructive-notice date that pre-dates any later applicant. **If fired**: spawn a new MP within 30 days; engage IP counsel; file the application using the first-use date recorded in the §8 evidence ledger.

**Trigger 2**. RWP™ reaches ≥100 public adopters, OR ≥1 external commercial-tier implementation.

> *Rationale*. Adoption volume and a commercial-tier external implementation are the points at which the cost of registration becomes small relative to the cost of an unprotected name. A commercial-tier external implementation in particular signals that third parties are deriving revenue from the protocol's reputation, which is the textbook condition under which federal registration is warranted. **If fired**: spawn a new MP at the next quarterly review; the trigger is not time-critical (no collision yet) but the cost-benefit has flipped.

**Trigger 3**. 12 months of public visible use have elapsed with no collision — positive validation signal.

> *Rationale*. A 12-month clean window is positive evidence that the name is durable, distinctive, and worth the federal-registration cost. Filing too early risks paying for a name that the market rejects; waiting beyond 12 months risks letting a deferred filing become a permanent omission. **If fired**: spawn a new MP within 60 days; treat as routine portfolio maintenance rather than incident response.

**Trigger 4**. An attempted infringement requires federal-court jurisdiction or treble damages that common-law ™ cannot deliver.

> *Rationale*. State-court remedies and cease-and-desist correspondence are the tools available under common-law rights. Federal-court jurisdiction, treble damages, and statutory damages are the tools available under federal registration. When an actual infringement requires the second toolkit, that is the moment the registration cost is justified. **If fired**: spawn a new MP immediately; the filing is part of the litigation strategy and is co-ordinated with counsel.

A YAKKL-internal calendar reminder at +6 months and +12 months from public launch (defined as the public availability of the `rhumbprotocol.com` and `rhumbprotocol.dev` domains plus the public RWP repository) forces a periodic review of whether any of the triggers above has fired. The calendar reminder is the operational backstop for the trigger language in this section; without it, a "we'll file eventually" pattern would be very easy to drift into.

## 9. Update Procedure

This `TRADEMARK.md` document is versioned independently of the Rhumb Workflow Protocol™ specification. The document version is recorded in the header at the top of this file. RWP protocol versions and `TRADEMARK.md` versions move on different cadences:

- **RWP protocol versions** advance whenever the protocol's normative artifacts (schemas, templates, grammar) change in a way that requires conformance suite updates.
- **`TRADEMARK.md` versions** advance whenever this policy text changes in a way that affects the rights or obligations of any party using the marks.

A change to this document requires:

1. A new internal MP that scopes the change, captures the rationale, and identifies any cross-document impact (for example, on the conformance gate in §6.2 if `rhumb-validate` semantics change).
2. A `CHANGELOG.md` entry under the appropriate version block, summarising what changed, citing the MP id, and (where relevant) noting any grace period or transitional behaviour.
3. A bump of the document version in this file's header.
4. Where the change affects the first-use claim in §4 or §5 — for example, a transition from common-law ™ to USPTO federal registration — an update to the private first-use evidence ledger to record the transition event with an ISO 8601 timestamp.

The policy in effect at any given time is the version published on the canonical branch of the public RWP repository at that time. Earlier versions remain in the git history; the canonical branch is authoritative for any current dispute.

Updates that are purely textual (typo fixes, formatting, link refresh) do not require a version bump; they require only a `CHANGELOG.md` line under the current version block noting the housekeeping touch.
